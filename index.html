Why this exists

        
An AI service desk sells on one promise: tickets stop arriving because agents resolve them. After go-live, the renewal depends on whether that promise keeps compounding. This console turns raw ticket and workflow-run data into three answers a Deployment Success Engineer needs every Monday:

        
    
          
  • Who is at risk, weighted by ARR and days to renewal.
    • 
          
  • What is broken, grouped by error signature so one code fix clears many accounts.
    • 
          
  • Where to grow: the next workflow to build inside a live department, and the next department to switch on.
    • 
        

        
Everything else (renewal brief, workflow scaffold, playbook queue) is generated from those answers so the motion scales without adding headcount in step with customers.

      

      

        
Health score (0–100)

        
health = 0.30·automation + 0.25·adoption
       + 0.15·momentum + 0.15·reliability
       + 0.15·sentiment

        
    
          
  • Automation: share of the last 4 weeks' tickets resolved with no human, scored against a 60% target.
    • 
          
  • Adoption: monthly unique requesters ÷ licensed seats, scored against 45%.
    • 
          
  • Momentum: automation rate, last 4 weeks vs first 4 of 12. Flat scores 50; +10 points scores 100.
    • 
          
  • Reliability: workflow run failure rate. 0% scores 100; 10% or more scores 0.
    • 
          
  • Sentiment: customer satisfaction (CSAT) on a 1–5 scale, 3.5 scores 0 and 4.8 scores 100.
    • 
        

        
Bands: 75+ healthy, 55–74 watch, under 55 at risk. An account is a priority when it is not healthy and renews within 120 days.

How I'd run it in production

      
    
        
  • Nightly job computes scores and posts the playbook queue to the deployment team's Slack channel; anything newly "at risk" pages the account owner.
    • 
        
  • New error signatures open an engineering ticket automatically with the request, response and affected tenants attached.
    • 
        
  • Renewal briefs are drafted 120, 90 and 30 days out, so account executives (AEs) walk into every renewal with numbers the customer recognizes.
    • 
        
  • Gap rankings feed working sessions with the customer's IT, HR, Finance and Legal teams: we arrive with the next three workflows already sized.
